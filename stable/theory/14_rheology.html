Non-Newtonian Rheology ​

Many fluids of practical interest — polymer solutions, blood, cement, food products — exhibit a viscosity that depends on the local deformation rate (shear-thinning or shear-thickening) or possess a yield stress. Kraken.jl provides a modular rheology framework that plugs into the LBM collision operator, with all models implemented as lightweight structs suitable for direct GPU kernel arguments.

References: Krüger et al. (2017) kruger2017lattice §12, Gabbanelli et al. (2005) gabbanelli2005lattice, Boyd et al. (2007) boyd2007analysis.

Generalized Newtonian fluids ​

A Generalized Newtonian Fluid (GNF) is defined by a viscosity that depends only on the local shear rate magnitude γ˙:

τ=2η(γ˙)S

where S=(∇u+∇uT)/2 is the strain-rate tensor and γ˙=2S:S is its second invariant.

In the LBM framework, the GNF approach modifies the collision step by computing a local relaxation rate at each node:

ω(x)=13ν(γ˙)+1/2

This is a purely local operation — no additional PDEs need to be solved.

Strain rate from non-equilibrium stress ​

A key advantage of the LBM is that the strain-rate tensor can be computed locally from the non-equilibrium part of the distributions, without any finite-difference stencil:

Παβneq=∑q(fq−fqeq)eqαeqβSαβ=−Παβneq2ρcs2τ

The shear rate magnitude is then:

γ˙=2SαβSαβ=2(Sxx2+Syy2+2Sxy2)(2D)

This is entirely local (no neighbour reads) and thus ideal for GPU execution. Both D2Q9 and D3Q19 implementations are provided:

julia
strain_rate_magnitude_2d(f1,...,f9, feq1,...,feq9, rho, tau) → γ̇
strain_rate_magnitude_3d(f1,...,f19, feq1,...,feq19, rho, tau) → γ̇

Implicit coupling

The strain rate depends on τ, which itself depends on γ˙ through the rheological model. In practice, a single explicit evaluation per time step (using τ from the previous step) is sufficient for stability, since the LBM time step is small.

D3Q19 TRT with LI-BB walls ​

fused_trt_gnf_libb_guo_field_step_3d! combines D3Q19 pull streaming, LI-BB cut links, local GNF relaxation and Guo forcing. This 3D path supports isothermal Newtonian, PowerLaw, CarreauYasuda and Cross models. Model parameters are converted to the population element type before launch; viscosity limits are those supplied by the constitutive model. The forcing convention follows guo2002discrete, with the even and odd Guo source terms weighted by their respective TRT factors 1−s+/2 and 1−s−/2.

The equilibrium velocity and corrected non-equilibrium moment are

u∗=∑qfqeq+F/2ρ,Παβ∗=∑q[fq−fqeq(ρ,u∗)]eqαeqβ+Fαuβ∗+uα∗Fβ2.

The previous even relaxation time enters the strain evaluation:

τ+old=1s+old,Sαβ=−3Παβ∗2ρτ+old,γ˙=2[Sxx2+Syy2+Szz2+2(Sxy2+Sxz2+Syz2)].

The new rates are stored per cell and used for the current collision. The channel driver keeps the TRT magic parameter fixed at Λ=3/16:

ν=ν(γ˙),s+=13ν+1/2,s−=1Λ/(3ν)+1/2.

run_gnf_channel_3d lowers planar walls at arbitrary cut fraction qw∈(0,1] with gap H=N−1+2qw. Body-force channels support any distinct flow and wall axes. The Zou-He option is restricted to x-directed flow with zero body force. It copies each open face's relaxation rates from the adjacent interior plane before rebuilding populations. This imposes a zero-normal-gradient closure for the rates: populations on an open face are incomplete before reconstruction, so their local strain is unsuitable for determining those boundary rates. Periodic dimensions include two ghost planes, refreshed for populations and both rate fields; reported profiles exclude ghosts, solid planes and Zou-He end faces.

Orders use the actual gap ratio, p=log⁡(E1/E2)/log⁡(H2/H1).

Power-law model ​

The simplest GNF model:

η(γ˙)=Kγ˙n−1

  • n<1: shear-thinning (e.g. polymer solutions, blood)

  • n>1: shear-thickening (e.g. dense suspensions)

  • n=1: Newtonian (η=K)

The singularity at γ˙→0 is regularized by clamping: ν∈[νmin,νmax].

julia
PowerLaw(K, n; nu_min=1e-6, nu_max=10.0)

Carreau-Yasuda model ​

A more physical model with finite zero-shear and infinite-shear viscosities:

η(γ˙)=η∞+(η0−η∞)[1+(λγ˙)a](n−1)/a

Setting a=2 recovers the standard Carreau model. The parameter λ is the relaxation time (inverse of the critical shear rate) and controls the onset of shear-thinning.

julia
CarreauYasuda(eta_0, eta_inf, lambda, a, n)

Cross model ​

An alternative to Carreau-Yasuda with a different functional form:

η(γ˙)=η∞+η0−η∞1+(Kγ˙)m
julia
Cross(eta_0, eta_inf, K, m)

Bingham plastic ​

A yield-stress fluid that behaves as a rigid solid below a critical stress τy and flows like a Newtonian fluid above it. The ideal Bingham model has a discontinuity at γ˙=0:

η(γ˙)=τyγ˙+μp(γ˙>0)

This is regularized using the Papanastasiou (1987) exponential approximation, which is branchless and GPU-safe:

η(γ˙)=τy1−exp⁡(−mγ˙)γ˙+μp

Larger m (regularization parameter) gives a closer approximation to ideal Bingham behaviour.

julia
Bingham(tau_y, mu_p; m_reg=1000.0)

Herschel-Bulkley model ​

Combines yield stress with power-law behaviour above yielding:

η(γ˙)=τy1−exp⁡(−mγ˙)γ˙+Kγ˙n−1

This generalizes both Bingham (n=1) and power-law (τy=0).

julia
HerschelBulkley(tau_y, K, n; m_reg=1000.0)

Thermal coupling ​

For temperature-dependent rheology, Kraken provides two shift models that multiply all rheological parameters by a thermal shift factor aT(T):

Arrhenius:

aT=exp[Ea(1T−1Tref)]

Williams-Landel-Ferry (WLF):

log⁡aT=−C1(T−Tref)C2+T−Tref

Both are implemented as lightweight structs with zero overhead when not used (IsothermalCoupling is eliminated at compile time):

julia
PowerLaw(K, n; thermal=ArrheniusCoupling(T_ref, E_a))
CarreauYasuda(eta_0, eta_inf, lambda, a, n; thermal=WLFCoupling(T_ref, C1, C2))

Viscoelastic models ​

For fluids with memory (polymer melts, Boger fluids), Kraken provides viscoelastic constitutive models that evolve a conformation tensor C alongside the LBM distributions.

Oldroyd-B ​

Linear viscoelastic model with a Newtonian solvent:

τp+λτp∇=2ηpS

where τp∇ is the upper-convected derivative and λ is the polymer relaxation time.

julia
OldroydB(nu_s, nu_p, lambda)

FENE-P ​

Finitely extensible nonlinear elastic model with Peterlin closure:

τp+λτp∇=f(trC)2ηpS

with the Peterlin function:

f(trC)=L2L2−trC

where L is the maximum extensibility. Reduces to Oldroyd-B as L→∞.

julia
FENEP(nu_s, nu_p, lambda, L_max)

Log-conformation formulation ​

LogConfFormulation() is the default of the OldroydB, FENEP and Saramito constructors; pass formulation=StressFormulation() to evolve the stress directly. See the next page for which driver uses which model.

At high Weissenberg numbers (Wi=λγ˙≫1), the conformation tensor can lose positive-definiteness, causing numerical blow-up. The log-conformation approach (Fattal & Kupferman, 2004) evolves Θ=log⁡C instead, which guarantees C remains positive-definite:

julia
OldroydB(nu_s, nu_p, lambda; formulation=LogConfFormulation())
FENEP(nu_s, nu_p, lambda, L_max; formulation=LogConfFormulation())

Implementation in Kraken ​

Type hierarchy ​

The rheology module uses Julia's type system for zero-overhead dispatch:

AbstractRheology
├── GeneralizedNewtonian
│   ├── Newtonian
│   ├── PowerLaw
│   ├── CarreauYasuda
│   ├── Cross
│   ├── Bingham
│   └── HerschelBulkley
└── Viscoelastic
    ├── OldroydB
    ├── FENEP
    └── Saramito

All structs are lightweight (no arrays) and passed directly as GPU kernel arguments. Julia's JIT specializes kernels per concrete type, so the dispatch is resolved at compile time with zero runtime overhead.

Collision kernel ​

The rheological collision kernel computes the local viscosity and relaxation rate at each node. It accepts generalized Newtonian models only; tau_field stores the relaxation time of the previous step. Viscoelastic models use the separate drivers of the next page. Variants with a Guo body force (collide_rheology_guo_2d!) and with a temperature field (collide_rheology_thermal_2d!) also exist:

julia
collide_rheology_2d!(f, is_solid, model::GeneralizedNewtonian, tau_field)

The algorithm at each node is:

  1. Compute macroscopic ρ, u from fq

  2. Compute equilibrium fqeq

  3. Compute γ˙ from Πneq

  4. Evaluate ν=effective_viscosity(model,γ˙)

  5. Compute ω=1/(3ν+1/2)

  6. BGK collision: fq⋆=fq−ω(fq−fqeq)

For thermal coupling, the variant effective_viscosity_thermal is used, which applies the shift factor aT before evaluating the model.

Thermally coupled collision ​

When the flow populations fq are coupled to a thermal double-distribution field gq (see thermal DDF), the kernel collide_rheology_thermal_2d!(f, is_solid, rheology, tau_field, Fx, Fy, Temp) is called after streaming. At each fluid node:

  1. The temperature is the zeroth moment of the thermal populations, T=∑qgq, stored in Temp by the streaming step.

  2. The Boussinesq force Fy=βg(T−Tref) (Fx=0) enters the velocity as the Guo half-force term, u=(∑qcqfq+F/2)/ρ.

  3. The shear rate γ˙ is evaluated from the non-equilibrium stress, using the relaxation time of the previous step.

  4. effective_viscosity_thermal(model, gamma_dot, T) multiplies the model's parameters by aT(T), then evaluates the model and applies the viscosity bounds of the model, if any.

  5. ω=1/(3ν+1/2) and τ=1/ω is stored in tau_field for the next step.

  6. BGK collision with Guo forcing, using this ω and the factor 1−ω/2 on the force term.

The driver run_natural_convection_gnf_2d assembles these steps for a differentially heated cavity; see Generalised Newtonian natural convection. Its fixed-temperature walls are the half-way anti-bounce-back of the Newtonian cavity (see thermal DDF), at the same place as the no-slip walls, so Ra, Pr and Nu use the cavity width H=N. With cavity_powerlaw, the reference viscosity ν0 is the nominal viscosity K(α/H2)n−1 of Turan et al. (2011), so Ra and Pr follow their power-law definitions. For a model without thermal coupling (aT=1), the viscosity still depends on γ˙ only, and temperature acts through the buoyancy force alone.
