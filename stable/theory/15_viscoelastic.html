Viscoelastic Constitutive Models ​

Viscoelastic fluids — polymer solutions, melts, Boger fluids — exhibit both viscous and elastic behaviour. Unlike Generalized Newtonian Fluids (which only modify the viscosity), viscoelastic models track an additional conformation tensor C (or equivalently the polymeric stress τp) that evolves alongside the LBM distributions.

References: Krüger et al. (2017) kruger2017lattice §12, Fattal & Kupferman (2004) fattal2004constitutive.

Total stress decomposition ​

The total deviatoric stress is split into a Newtonian solvent contribution and a polymeric contribution:

τ=2ηsS+τp

where ηs=ρνs is the solvent viscosity. The LBM collision handles the solvent part (via the relaxation rate ωs=1/(3νs+1/2)), while τp is evolved by a separate PDE and coupled to the LBM through a body force.

Oldroyd-B model ​

The simplest viscoelastic model couples a Newtonian solvent with a single-mode upper-convected Maxwell element:

τp+λτp∇=2ηpS

where τp∇ is the upper-convected derivative:

τp∇=∂τp∂t+u⋅∇τp−τp⋅∇u−(∇u)T⋅τp

λ is the polymer relaxation time and ηp=ρνp the polymer viscosity. The Weissenberg number Wi=λγ˙ controls the degree of viscoelasticity.

julia
OldroydB(nu_s, nu_p, lambda)

FENE-P model ​

The Oldroyd-B model allows unlimited polymer stretching, which is unphysical. The Finitely Extensible Nonlinear Elastic model with Peterlin closure (FENE-P) introduces a maximum extensibility L:

τp=Gf(trC)(C−I)

with the Peterlin function:

f(trC)=L2L2−trC

where G=νp/λ is the elastic modulus and C is the conformation tensor. The Oldroyd-B model is recovered as L→∞.

julia
FENEP(nu_s, nu_p, lambda, L_max)

Saramito model ​

The Saramito (2007) model extends Oldroyd-B with a yield stress, combining viscoelasticity with viscoplasticity. Below the yield stress τy, the material behaves as a Kelvin-Voigt viscoelastic solid; above it, as an Oldroyd-B fluid. This is achieved by modifying the relaxation term with a regularized yield function.

julia
Saramito(nu_s, nu_p, lambda, tau_y; m_reg=1000.0)

Log-conformation formulation ​

At high Weissenberg numbers (Wi≫1), the conformation tensor C can lose positive-definiteness due to exponential growth of eigenvalues, causing numerical blow-up. The log-conformation approach of fattal2004constitutive evolves Θ=log⁡C instead:

∂Θ∂t+u⋅∇Θ=ΩΘ−ΘΩ+2B+1λ(fe−Θ−I)

where Ω and B come from decomposing the velocity gradient ∇u in the eigenbasis of C=exp⁡(Θ).

Since Θ is unconstrained (any symmetric matrix), C=exp⁡(Θ) is guaranteed positive-definite, eliminating the most common source of instability in viscoelastic simulations.

julia
OldroydB(nu_s, nu_p, lambda; formulation=LogConfFormulation())
FENEP(nu_s, nu_p, lambda, L_max; formulation=LogConfFormulation())

Eigendecomposition and matrix operations ​

The log-conformation formulation requires efficient 2×2 symmetric matrix operations (eigendecomposition, exp, log) at every lattice node. Kraken provides GPU-optimized, branchless implementations:

julia
eigen_sym2x2(a11, a12, a22)       # → (λ1, λ2, e1x, e1y, e2x, e2y)
mat_exp_sym2x2(a11, a12, a22)     # → (e11, e12, e22)
mat_log_sym2x2(a11, a12, a22)     # → (l11, l12, l22)
decompose_velocity_gradient(...)   # → (Ω12, B11, B22)

All use analytical closed-form expressions with atan-based eigenvector computation, avoiding branches that would cause GPU warp divergence.

Which code path uses which model ​

Two implementations coexist in src/:

  • The explicit-Euler upwind kernels described in the rest of this page (evolve_stress_2d!, evolve_logconf_2d!, compute_polymeric_force_2d!) are driven by run_viscoelastic_cylinder_2d, which selects the formulation with a symbol (formulation=:logconf), not with the OldroydB/FENEP structs above.

  • The production 2D and 3D drivers (run_viscoelastic_logfv_*, run_viscoelastic_fvfd_* and the .krk viscoelastic dispatch in simulation_runner.jl) evolve the log-conformation on a finite-volume grid, with MUSCL-superbee advection by default, and take a polymer closure of type LogConfOldroydB, LogConfFENEP, LogConfGiesekus or LogConfPTT (src/drivers/viscoelastic_spec.jl). The 3D sphere driver only accepts OldroydBSpec (Oldroyd-B in direct conformation form); it refuses log-conformation models.

  • Saramito exists as a type and can be built from a .krk file, but no viscoelastic driver consumes it yet.

Coupling with LBM ​

The polymeric stress is coupled to the LBM collision through the Guo forcing scheme. At each time step:

  1. Compute macroscopic ρ, u from distributions

  2. Evolve τp (or Θ) using upwind advection + source + relaxation (explicit Euler)

  3. Compute the polymeric force as the divergence of the stress:

Fp,i=∂τp,ij∂xj
  1. Add Fp to the Guo forcing term in the BGK collision

The force divergence is computed with central differences:

julia
compute_polymeric_force_2d!(Fx_p, Fy_p, tau_xx, tau_xy, tau_yy)

Stress vs. log-conformation kernels ​

Two evolution kernels are available, selected by the formulation field:

FormulationEvolvesStabilityCost
StressFormulation()τp directlyWi ≲ 1Lower
LogConfFormulation()Θ=log⁡CWi ≫ 1Eigendecomposition
julia
evolve_stress_2d!(tau_xx_new, tau_xy_new, tau_yy_new,
                   tau_xx, tau_xy, tau_yy, ux, uy, nu_p, lambda)

evolve_logconf_2d!(Theta_xx_new, Theta_xy_new, Theta_yy_new,
                    Theta_xx, Theta_xy, Theta_yy, ux, uy;
                    lambda=1.0, L_max=0.0)

Both are fused advection + source + relaxation GPU kernels: a single kernel launch per time step for all three tensor components.
