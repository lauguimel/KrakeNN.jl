Thermal Physics API ​

Thermal physics APIs provide the DDF temperature kernels, Boussinesq coupling, and natural-convection drivers for 2D and 3D workflows. Thermal refinement helpers are listed below for reference; Grid refinement (patch and conservative AMR) is under development and not validated; it is not part of the supported feature set.

Kraken.apply_fixed_temp_2d! Method
julia
apply_fixed_temp_2d!(g, Nx, Ny; west=nothing, east=nothing,
                     south=nothing, north=nothing)

Fixed temperatures on several faces at once by half-way anti-bounce-back (see apply_fixed_temp_west_2d! for a single face). A face is Dirichlet when its keyword is a number. Where two Dirichlet faces share a corner, the diagonal slot whose link crosses both walls is flipped once, with the mean of the two wall temperatures; one face alone gives the same result as its per-face function. g must be the post-stream array of a bounce-back stream kernel; call once per step, after streaming and before collision.

julia
using Kraken

g = zeros(Float64, 8, 8, 9)
apply_fixed_temp_2d!(g, 8, 8; west=1.0, south=1.0, east=0.0, north=0.0)

Kraken.collide_boussinesq_vt_2d_kernel! Method
julia
collide_boussinesq_vt_2d!(f, Temp, is_solid, ν_ref, T_ref, A_arr, β_g)

BGK collision with per-node Boussinesq force AND temperature-dependent viscosity. ν(T) = ν_ref · exp(A_arr · (1/T - 1/T_ref)) (Arrhenius law) ω(T) = 1 / (3·ν(T) + 0.5)

Kraken.collide_boussinesq_vt_modified_2d_kernel! Method
julia
collide_boussinesq_vt_modified_2d!(f, Temp, is_solid, ν_ref, T0_visc, α_visc, β_g, T_ref_buoy)

BGK collision with per-node Boussinesq force AND temperature-dependent viscosity. ν(T) = ν_ref · exp(α_visc · (T - T0_visc)) (modified Arrhenius / Frank-Kamenetskii) ω(T) = 1 / (3·ν(T) + 0.5) Buoyancy: Fy = β_g · (T - T_ref_buoy)

Used for rheological contrast studies: α_visc = ln(Rc), Rc = η_max/η_min.

Kraken.run_natural_convection_2d Method
julia
run_natural_convection_2d(; N=128, Ra=1e4, Pr=0.71, Rc=1.0,
                            T_hot=1.0, T_cold=0.0, max_steps=50000,
                            backend, FT)

Natural convection in a square cavity: hot left wall, cold right wall, adiabatic top/bottom. Boussinesq approximation with optional temperature-dependent viscosity via modified Arrhenius law. All walls are half-way (half a lattice spacing outside the first and last nodes), so the thermal and fluid cavities share the width H = N. The returned Nu is the hot-wall average over rows 2:N-1 of the second-order one-sided gradient through the wall value and the first two nodes.

Ra = β·g·ΔT·H³/(ν·α), Pr = ν/α, Rc = η_max/η_min (rheological contrast). For Rc=1: constant viscosity. For Rc>1: ν(T) = ν_ref·exp(α_visc·(T - T_ref)).

Kraken.run_natural_convection_3d Method
julia
run_natural_convection_3d(; N=32, Ra=1e3, Pr=0.71,
                            T_hot=1.0, T_cold=0.0, max_steps=10000,
                            backend, FT)

Natural convection in a cube: hot west wall, cold east wall, adiabatic on all other faces. All walls are half-way (half a lattice spacing outside the first and last nodes), so the cavity width is H = N for both flow and heat. D3Q19 lattice with Boussinesq forcing in the y-direction (gravity pointing -y).

Reference: Fusegi et al. (1991) — Ra=1e3 → Nu≈1.085, Ra=1e4 → Nu≈2.10.

julia
run_natural_convection_refined_2d(; N=128, Ra=1e4, Pr=0.71, Rc=1.0,
                                    T_hot=1.0, T_cold=0.0, max_steps=50000,
                                    wall_fraction=0.2, ratio=2,
                                    backend, FT)

Natural convection in a square cavity with patch refinement near the hot (west) and cold (east) walls. Patches cover wall_fraction of the domain width on each side, refined by ratio (default 2:1).

Same physics as run_natural_convection_2d. Grid refinement is under development and not validated (refined thermal walls are not half-way, issue #98).

Kraken.run_rayleigh_benard_2d Method
julia
run_rayleigh_benard_2d(; Nx=128, Ny=32, Ra=2000, Pr=1.0, T_hot=1.0, T_cold=0.0,
                        max_steps=20000, nu=nothing, alpha=nothing,
                        orientation=:vertical, backend, FT)

Rayleigh-Bénard convection: hot bottom wall, cold top wall, periodic x. Ra = β·g·ΔT·H³/(ν·α), Pr = ν/α.

Material parameters:

  • nu: kinematic viscosity in lattice units. nothing keeps the historical default 0.05, chosen so that ω stays away from 2.

  • alpha: thermal diffusivity in lattice units. nothing keeps the historical ν / Pr. When alpha is given it wins, and the returned Pr is the effective ν / α rather than the requested one.

Orientation:

  • :vertical (default): temperature is imposed on south (T_hot) and north (T_cold), x is periodic. This is Rayleigh-Bénard proper and is the historical behaviour.

  • :horizontal: temperature is imposed on west (T_hot) and east (T_cold), all four faces are solid (bounce-back). Gravity still acts along y, so at large Ra this is a differentially heated cavity; at Ra → 0 it is pure conduction across x.

Any other orientation is rejected rather than silently replaced.

Kraken._fill_thermal_ghost_simple! Method

Bilinear interpolation of coarse g into fine ghost cells.

Kraken._fill_thermal_ghost_temporal! Method

Temporal + bilinear interpolation of coarse g into fine ghost cells, with Filippova-Hanel rescaling of the non-equilibrium part.

Formula (at each ghost cell): g_fine[q] = g_eq(q, T_f, u_f) + α_c2f · Σ w · (g_corner[q] - g_eq(q, T_corner, u_corner))

where T_corner, u_corner are temporally blended between time n (prev) and n+1 (curr), and T_f, u_f are the bilinear interpolations of those corner values.

Kraken.fill_thermal_ghost! Method
julia
fill_thermal_ghost!(patch, thermal, g_coarse, ux_coarse, uy_coarse,
                    Nx_c, Ny_c; t_frac=0)

Fill fine-grid thermal ghost cells with bilinear + temporal interpolation AND Filippova–Hänel rescaling of the non-equilibrium part (α_c2f = ratio). While g_eq is linear in T, g_neq carries the heat flux and must be rescaled; without this, the thermal flux delivered to the patch is under-estimated by a factor ratio, which biases Nu high (observed +6–9 % on De Vahl Davis). When t_frac &gt; 0, temporally interpolates between saved g_prev (time n) and current g_coarse (time n+1) for sub-cycling consistency.
